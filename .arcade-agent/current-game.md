# Active game

**stormhook** — `stormhook/`

started: 2026-08-27

## Pitch

*Stormhook* is a side-view momentum-traversal action platformer: a physics
grapple game in the Bionic Commando / Umihara Kawase lineage, with original IP.
You are a sky salvager working the wreck-fields above a drowned foundry world,
armed with a magnetic tether that latches onto anything ferrous — derelict
airship hulls, swinging chain-anchors, drifting cargo plates. You almost never
touch the ground. You fire the line, swing, pay it out to drop under a hull,
reel it in to slingshot over one, and release at the top of the arc to fly. A
storm front advances behind you the whole time, so hesitation is punished and
the correct answer is always *keep your momentum*. Levels are ended by reaching
the extraction beacon; score is salvage collected multiplied by an airtime combo
that only survives while your boots stay off solid geometry.

The verb is what makes it worth building: **aim a point, latch, swing, release**
is one of the very few action-game control schemes that is genuinely *better*
with a touchscreen than with a gamepad — you point at the thing you want to
grab. On desktop it is mouse-aim plus A/D lean and a reel on W/S. No virtual
d-pad, no twitch button rows. That solves the one problem that makes most
platformers bad on a phone.

## Why this game, and why now

Every genre currently in the arcade is either static-camera or combat-driven.
There is **no platformer of any kind** in the project — no game whose core verb
is jumping, swinging, or traversing a scrolling level. That is the single
largest gap in the catalog, and the platformer is the most iconic console genre
there is (*Super Mario World* is the best-selling SNES game ever made). This
fills that hole without being a Mario reskin: the hook is rope physics and
conserved momentum, not run-and-jump.

Per the 80/20 classic-to-novel mix, this sits on the **classic** side — grapple
platformers are a real, established, decades-old genre (*Roc'n Rope* 1983,
*Bionic Commando* 1987, *Umihara Kawase* 1994) — with an original setting and
an original scoring loop rather than a licensed clone.

The AAA reference bar for its critics: **Ori and the Blind Forest** and
**Rayman Legends** for art, lighting and parallax; **Umihara Kawase Fresh!** and
*Bionic Commando Rearmed* for rope feel; **Tetris Effect** for the standard of
audio-reactive VFX.
