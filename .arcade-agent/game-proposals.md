## 1. Whiteout Run — mountain stunt adventure

**Foundation:** classic SSX-style snowboard racing and trick mastery with original
characters/world. Fantasy: a courier races the last rescue train across a mountain
range, restoring a chain of weather stations before the valley is buried.

**Core loop and twist:** choose a route, carve downhill, chain tricks and grind
rails to build heat; spend heat to open frozen shortcuts or rescue stranded
climbers. Banking a trick combo creates a heat ribbon that briefly stabilizes
wind or crumbling terrain for the next section. Scoring and survival compete.

**Systems:** momentum/carving/braking; jump/spin/grab/landing combos; heat resource
and environmental routes; rescue cargo that changes handling; gear unlocks and
optional route records. Third-person/pseudo-3D downhill presentation on canvas.

**Five authored venues / twenty distinct events:**
- Pineglass Basin: carving gates, first rail chain, cabin rescue, forest descent.
- Cabletown: roof transfer, moving gondola jump, cable grind, rooftop delivery.
- Blue Cathedral: ice-wall traverse, crevasse transfer, cave slalom, beacon repair.
- Storm Crown: crosswind ridge, avalanche escape, heat-shortcut trial, summit race.
- Last Light Pass: supply relay, train interception, multi-route rescue, final
  descent combining storm, cargo, heat and trick systems with a valley evacuation.

**Progression and scoring:** persistent venue unlocks and board/heat upgrades,
checkpoint/save between events, medal goals and route discoveries; campaign score
combines tricks, rescues and event results. Finale pays off the weather-network arc.

**Controls:** keyboard steer/brake/jump plus trick modifier; touch steering pad,
brake, jump and context-sensitive trick input. Controls must support simultaneous
steer/trick without covering the landing zone.

**Direction:** cold blue ice, warm signal lamps, readable rider silhouette,
procedural snow/ridges/town structures; wind, edge scrape and evolving rhythmic
music. Real-speed landing and mobile camera clarity are critical.

**Two-week sequence:** first playable slope/handling/tricks; then Pineglass and
Cabletown; heat/rescue/progression; Blue Cathedral and Storm Crown; Last Light
and ending; full-tour input checks; independent visual/UI/audio/VFX/gameplay
critics and focused revisions. Do not call one slope the finished tour.

**Main risk:** convincing speed/landing feedback and fair mobile visibility on
authored branching slopes. Must prove handling early, before adding 20 events.

## 2. Signalbreak — squad tactics campaign

**Foundation:** classic turn-based squad tactics in the XCOM/Fire Emblem lineage,
with original non-alien setting. Fantasy: lead four signal engineers through an
occupied city, freeing districts by rerouting the network that controls them.

**Core loop and twist:** scout, position a small squad, exploit cover/elevation,
complete an objective and extract. An engineer can rewire a power circuit so
lights, lifts, doors and enemy turrets change allegiance together. One network
choice can create a route and expose a flank elsewhere; it is not a damage spell.

**Systems:** turn/action-point movement and clear attack previews; cover/line of
sight/overwatch; hackable shared power networks; squad classes/loadouts and injury
recovery; optional rescues and campaign resource choices. No required real-time
aiming, and every move should be readable before committing.

**Five authored districts / twenty distinct operations:**
- Switchyard: cover extraction, prisoner release, substation isolation, rail ambush.
- Vertical Market: rooftop crossfire, cargo-lift escort, clinic defense, antenna raid.
- Floodworks: sluice routing, moving bridge capture, blackout infiltration, pump siege.
- Glass Archive: evidence recovery, split-squad access, decoy transmission, convoy stop.
- Broadcast Citadel: outer relay, multi-floor breach, civilian corridor defense,
  core shutdown finale with consequences from rescued staff and recovered evidence.

**Progression and scoring:** squad abilities and equipment, persistent district
state, save at mission boundaries, clear campaign narrative with recurring radio
characters. Score rewards objectives, rescues, efficient turns and squad survival.
Optional medals/replays support mastery without making restart mandatory.

**Controls:** click/tap a unit then destination/target, action preview and explicit
confirm; keyboard selection/action shortcuts. Large touch targets and undoable
movement previews keep the tactical field readable on a phone.

**Direction:** isometric industrial dioramas, restrained amber/cyan electrical
language, visible circuit routes and actor silhouettes; radio callouts, electrical
motifs and reactive combat music. Original identity; no borrowed XCOM assets.

**Two-week sequence:** small playable combat/hacking encounter; full tactical
rules/previews and class roles; first two districts; progression/save and Floodworks;
Archive/Citadel/finale; campaign solvability and touch path; critics and revisions.

**Main risk:** AI, cover/LOS fairness and interaction complexity. Start with a
compact reliable ruleset; create depth through authored situations, not dozens
of poorly tested abilities.

## 3. The Ninth Passenger — interconnected detective adventure

**Foundation:** novel deduction adventure informed by the evidence-driven design
of Return of the Obra Dinn, with original setting and mechanics. Fantasy: an
inspector boards an overnight train whose passengers all remember a different
journey; identify the missing ninth passenger before the terminus disappears.

**Core loop and twist:** inspect carriages, interview witnesses, compare physical
evidence and reconstruct a timeline. A mechanical timetable lets you view each
carriage at three recorded stops. Objects persist or move between stops; matching
their trajectories reveals contradictions, secret routes and false identities.
Evidence is authored and visual; no generated dialogue or opaque guessing gates.

**Systems:** explorable scene hotspots/inventory; evidence/timeline board; witness
claims and contradiction deductions; cross-carriage object trajectories; optional
reconstructions that change the final accusation. No reflex combat or timer pressure.

**Five authored chapters / twenty substantial deductions:**
- Sleeper: ticket ownership, impossible wake-up, switched luggage, hidden compartment.
- Dining Car: seating chronology, poisoned service route, duplicate reservation,
  witness contradiction.
- Observation: photograph location, signal timing, broken reflection, roof crossing.
- Mail/Engine: parcel chain, forged dispatch, brake event, engineer identity.
- Vanished Carriage: missing seat, shared alibi, complete route reconstruction,
  ninth-passenger identification and an evidence-backed final decision/epilogue.

Each deduction needs multiple independent clues and a unique reasoning step;
twenty locked drawers or repeated matching puzzles do not meet the scope.

**Progression and scoring:** journal autosave, unlocked carriage routes and verified
deductions, optional passenger histories, chapter transitions and a complete ending.
Score reflects supported deductions and optional evidence, with explicit hint-use
deductions; no speed leaderboard that encourages skipping the story.

**Controls:** click/tap to inspect, large journal/timeline tabs, drag or select-pair
evidence linking with a keyboard-accessible alternative. Responsive scene framing.

**Direction:** cutaway night train, brass mechanisms, ink-and-paper casebook,
distinct passenger silhouettes and lamp-lit scenes. Procedural scene illustration
with train rhythm, compartment ambience and restrained mystery motifs.

**Two-week sequence:** one fully authored deduction with timeline UI; clue schema
and validation; Sleeper/Dining; Observation/Mail; vanished carriage and finale;
fresh-player clue review, full solve/hint paths and save/restore; critics/revisions.

**Main risk:** writing twenty fair, interlocking deductions. Lock the solution graph
and clue dependencies before illustration; automated solvability is not evidence
that fresh players understand the mystery.
